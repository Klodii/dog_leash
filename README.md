# Dog leash
How to create a dog leash that can be shortened at will.

<img src="imgs/00_leash.jpg" alt="dog_leash" width="50%"/>

## What you need
<img src="imgs/0_required.jpg" alt="requirements" width="50%"/>

- 360 degree swivel carabiner
- 6 mm diameter rope — this will be the main leash
- 4 mm diameter rope — used to shorten the leash

> **Note**: I suggest to take the ropes of two different colors.
> This will make them easier to identify when reacing to shorten the leash.

### Lengths
- **6 mm rope**: You can make it long as you like, but keep in mind that knots
  will reduce the total length by approximately **80 cm**.
  For example, if you start with a 5 m rope, the final length will be around
  **4.20 m** after tying the necessary knots.
- **4 mm rope**: If you want a compact setup, a **60 cm** rope is enough. For
  more flexibility, I recomend using **90 cm**.


## Build
### 1. Attach the Carabiner with a Scaffold Knot
Create the first knot at the base of the leash using a **Scaffold knot**.\
This knot will pass through the carabiner. Be sure to leave a few centimeters of
excess rope for safety.

The Scaffold knot tightens under tension and will effectively “weld” into position.

> **Note:** With the excess rope, you can tie a **Double Overhand Stopper Knot**.\
> This keeps the excess rope close to the main rope and improves the visual appearance.\
> (Also, the Double Overhand Knot just looks good.)

We chose this knot instead of a Bowline + stopper combo because the Scaffold
knot has a smaller footprint, which reduces the weight at that point — important
for the dog’s comfort.

<img src="imgs/1_scaffold_carabiner.jpg" alt="scaffold_carabiner" width="50%"/>

---

### 2. Create the Handle with a Scotts Locked Bowline
For the handle, use the **Scotts Locked Bowline** — a variation of the classic
Bowline that’s safer and prevents the rope from slipping.

As always, leave some extra rope at the end for safety.

> **Note**: Previously, we used a classic Bowline with a Strangle Knot as a
> stopper. However, over time, the handle would shrink due to the dog pulling.
> The Scotts Locked Bowline solves this by staying secure under tension.

<img src="imgs/2_scotts_locked_bowline.jpg" alt="scotts_locked_bowline" width="50%"/>

---

### 3. Add the Prusic Knot for Adjustability
Using the **4 mm rope**, create a **Prusik Knot** around the main leash.\
This is a type of slide-and-grip knot (similar to a triple Cow Hitch) that
allows you to shorten or lengthen the leash.

Wrap the rope around the main leash **at least three times** — more turns will
provide more grip.

<img src="imgs/3_prusik_knot_slide_and_grip.jpg" alt="prusik_knot" width="50%"/>

---

### 4. Attach the Small Rope to the Handle Loop

Tie the remaining ends of the small rope to the Bowline loop you created in step
2.\
Use a **Double or Triple Fisherman's Bend**, passing the loop of the handle
inside before finishing the knot.

> **Note**: The more turns you make with the Fisherman’s Bend, the more secure
> it will be. We recommend using a **Triple Fisherman's Bend** for maximum hold.

<img src="imgs/4_triple_fishermans_bend.jpg" alt="triple_fisherman_bend" width="50%"/>

# Herringbone Dog Leash

**Herringbone leashes** are made by interweaving ropes in a decorative pattern.\
They are generally more ornamental than the functional leash described above.
